Method overview · CAS for IoMT attack attribution

How Clause-Activation Signatures turn a Tsetlin Machine into an auditable attributor

A trained weighted Tsetlin Machine already contains 2,400 clauses per model. CAS asks which of them can be trusted as evidence for each attack family, signs that evidence for and against, and certifies both how much of it could be spurious and how far it can be trusted when families mix.

2 · The sign fix, and why it has to be there

Stability selection alone tells you a clause is reliably associated with a family. It does not tell you which direction. Step 05a splits every stably-selected clause by the sign of its mean fitted coefficient, matching Fig. 2 of the paper.

S⁺ — Inculpatory

Mean coefficient > 0. Firing raises the fitted log-odds of family ℓ, so it is added to the score with weight π̂ⱼ.

↓ combine
S⁻ — Exculpatory

Mean coefficient < 0. Firing lowers the fitted log-odds of ℓ, so it is subtracted with weight π̂ⱼ instead of ignored.

↓ normalize by Σ π̂ⱼ over S⁺
sℓ(x) — the attribution score
sℓ(x) = ΣS⁺ π̂ⱼ zⱼ(x) − ΣS⁻ π̂ⱼ zⱼ(x) ΣS⁺ π̂ⱼ
Why this is the correct construction, not a heuristic: in the ℓ-versus-rest logistic fit that CPSS runs internally, a clause's coefficient sign is exactly its direction of association by that model's own definition. Summing every selected clause with the same sign, as a naive scorer does, adds an against-vote as if it were a for-vote whenever an exculpatory clause fires.
